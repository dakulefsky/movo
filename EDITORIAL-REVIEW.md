# Editorial review — handbook revision 0.2.0

This is a record of checks by the same editor, not an independent Torah review.

## Structural correction

The user's approved model is concrete subjects with concise explanatory subheadings: “olah is ...; asham is ...”. The revision uses 15 entries and 61 short blocks. The first screen is olah rather than an essay opening. Each block has its own references. The policy is recorded for every future area, but only Kodashim is implemented.

## Source checks and qualifications

- Re-read the actual source passages used to rewrite the offerings, rather than rearranging the previous essay without review. The Introduction to Kodashim remains primary; the translator's preface is explicitly separate.
- Olah: separated obligatory examples from voluntary olah, male animal offerings from birds of either sex, and altar flesh from the priest's hide. Bird service was checked against Mishnah Zevachim 6:5 and Rambam there, distinguishing the upper application from animal olah.
- Chattat: distinguished ordinary fixed sin offerings, variable means, associated purification offerings, eaten animal sin offerings, burned inner offerings and bird sin offerings. The bird's lack of altar flesh was checked against Mishnah 6:4 and Rambam. The listed exceptions prevent treating every chattat as the same gender, animal or purpose.
- Asham: all six named kinds appear together, with the triggering circumstances supported by the introduction and Mishnah. Kept the limitation on uncertain liability; the example is the actual fat/permitted-fat case in Mishnah Keritot 4:1. Did not turn every doubt into asham talui. Highlighted the hand/tool split of the metzora's asham blood, explicitly stated in Rambam Zevachim 5:1.
- Shelamim: distinguished ordinary owner eating from public shelamim; fixed public shelamim as the two Shavuot lambs. Explicitly retained the distinct eating periods of todah/nazir and ordinary shelamim.
- Todah: verified forty loaves, four types and four priestly loaves against Rambam Menachot 7:1. Avoided confusing the four priestly loaves with the total number.
- Nazir: distinguished the ram at completion of pure nezirut from the asham upon impurity; listed the priest's cooked foreleg and bread according to Rambam 5:6.
- Bekhor: identified the biblical statement as a biblical source, alongside Rambam's treatment of bekhor as a property-related obligation. Did not derive expanded eligibility laws from the short verse.
- Animal tithe: used the actual counting procedure, and identified the dispute over selecting one of ten; Rambam's conclusion is stated separately from the dissent.
- Pesach: retained the limited treatment of the midnight question. Meiri is used only for his actual explanation of the scope and organization of Pesachim, expressly under his name.
- Minchah remains an explicitly limited introduction. The dispute about a sinful priest's meal offering is identified, along with Rambam's ruling; the missing baraita at the end of the saved Menachot 1:1 passage was not reconstructed.
- Shared rules distinguish a valid offering from fulfillment, piggul from notar, qualified intent from an arbitrary person's intent, and shechitah from priestly service. Kept the chattat-for-chullin qualification.
- Genuine source difficulties remain visible in source notes: “niddah” versus the later “zav/ zavah” language in the introduction, and the parentheses/brackets in Rambam Zevachim 2:1. No corruption ruling or silent correction was introduced.

No claim of comprehensive coverage or publication readiness is made. Citation resolution, package checks and browser tests are technical checks, not proof of interpretation.
